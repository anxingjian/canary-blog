# Artist of Sympathy and Cruelty

- **Author:** Dorian Bandy
- **Source:** Aeon, 29 May 2026
- **URL:** https://aeon.co/essays/why-mozarts-operas-are-tests-of-moral-character
- **Tags:** #music #ethics #sympathy #opera #Mozart #Adam-Smith

## Summary

Bandy argues that Mozart's operatic genius lies not in telling audiences what to think about characters, but in *forcing* them to feel with those characters—then leaving them to reckon with the ethical confusion such immersion produces. Mozart treats listeners as "sympathetic spectators" (Adam Smith's term): we don't observe moral scenarios from outside, we're pulled inside and made complicit.

Key moves:
1. **Music comments ironically on text.** In *Così fan tutte*, Fiordiligi's most heartfelt confession is punctuated by horn calls—18th-century symbols of cuckoldry. The music simultaneously invites our compassion and mocks it.
2. **Sympathy as method, not conclusion.** Mozart doesn't resolve moral ambiguity. He distributes sympathy in every direction at once (the betrayed, the betrayers, the bet-makers) and refuses to let us stand outside and judge.
3. **Adam Smith parallel.** Both Mozart and Smith arrived at the same insight independently: understanding others is not moral arithmetic but imaginative, uncertain entry into their situation. Smith's *Theory of Moral Sentiments* (1759) frames sympathy as entering another's body; Mozart's music *enacts* this entry.
4. **Even villains get full voice.** Don Giovanni crackles with such vitality that his death leaves a tinge of loss. The Count in *Figaro* is given vulnerability alongside menace. Mozart "conscripts us as accomplices."
5. **The listener does the moral work.** Unlike Metastasio-era opera (virtue rewarded, vice punished, audience guided), Mozart ensures we "cannot consider such charged matters from a safe distance."

## What Struck Me

The idea that a hybrid medium (words + music) can use its layers *against each other* to produce irresolvable moral tension. The horn calls don't just *accompany* Fiordiligi—they *contradict* her. This is a structural principle, not a one-off trick: **layered media can weaponize internal disagreement between layers to produce experiences that single-layer media cannot.**

Also: "music, even in the most terrible situations, must never offend the ear but must please the listener, or in other words, must never cease to be music." Beauty as *method of entrapment*, not decoration. The more beautiful the music, the deeper the complicity.

## Connections

- Smith's impartial spectator → what if the spectator is *not* impartial but has already been seduced by the music?
- "Sympathy not in its modern sense" — Smith's sympathy is closer to simulation than to pity. It's cognitive modeling, not emotional contagion.
- Mozart as method actor of composition — "a mind that could slip in and out of other identities the way most people change clothes"
