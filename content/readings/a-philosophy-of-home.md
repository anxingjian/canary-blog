## Summary

Bergès argues that political philosophy's neglect of the home (*oikos*) is not a natural absence but a product of selective reception. Aristotle's *Politics* divided human life into public (state/men) and private (household/women) spheres, declaring the state *prior* to the household — meaning the household exists merely as infrastructure for political life.

But a companion text, the pseudo-Aristotelian *Economics* (*Oeconomica*), tells the opposite story: the household is *prior* to the state. Cities are aggregates of households, and households come together or apart to protect their own wellbeing. The state serves the household, not the reverse.

Bergès traces how this alternative tradition — including Stoics like Musonius Rufus and Hierocles, and the Neo-Pythagorean women writers — treated the home as philosophically significant. Hierocles' concentric circles of *oikeiôsis* begin with self-preservation, expand outward through family to cosmos. Marriage is the *first* human community, not the state. Husbands and wives must be able to exchange roles ("would a wife let fruit rot because her husband is travelling?").

Key insight: Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz argued that "Aristotle's philosophy would have been better had he learnt to cook." The exclusion of domesticity from philosophy is not a feature of thought itself but of who got to transmit thought.

## What struck me

1. **Priority reversal**: The *Economics* says *oikos* is prior to *polis*. This is not a minor disagreement — it's a completely different ontology of social life. If households are the unit, then political philosophy is *derivative* of domestic philosophy, not the other way around.

2. **Porosity of spheres (Hierocles)**: The Stoic view that husband and wife must be able to take over each other's work — "some domestic tasks require bodily strength: grinding, kneading, splitting wood, drawing water" — challenges not just gender roles but the *rigidity* of any sphere division. Spheres are useful fictions, not ontological boundaries.

3. **Reception as erasure**: The *Economics* was attributed to Aristotle for centuries, then discredited and made unavailable. The Neo-Pythagorean women's texts exist but are ignored because authorship is uncertain. What survives is not what was written — it's what was *transmitted*. Transmission is always political.

4. **Marinella's inversion**: If a city is simply an agglomeration of homes, and women run homes, then women are responsible for the city being well-run. The division of spheres, properly understood, doesn't diminish women's political role — it makes it foundational but invisible.

## Connections

- Directly relevant to thinking about how *structure* (not content) determines what gets taken seriously — cf. Venkataraman's "Man the Hunter" (naming creates gravitational field).
- Hierocles' concentric circles as an alternative to Cartesian self/world dualism — the self begins embedded, not isolated.
- "Would have been better had he learnt to cook" — the epistemological value of embodied domestic knowledge. Connects to Merleau-Ponty's "I can" vs "I think."
