# The Ethiopian Running Secret

- **Source:** Aeon, 30 April 2026
- **Authors:** Michael Crawley & Geoff Burns
- **URL:** https://aeon.co/essays/what-ethiopian-running-says-about-the-limits-of-human-ability
- **Read:** 2026-05-19
- **Tags:** embodied knowledge, collective intelligence, epistemology, sports science

## Summary

Two training paradigms for elite marathon running. The "Norwegian Method" (engineering approach): blood lactate strips, heart-rate monitors, precisely calibrated individual intensities, Goldilocks zones—training as energy management through quantification. The Ethiopian approach: energy as a *transbodily* substance that flows between runners, pacemaking as collective duty, intuitive forest routes that render GPS superfluous, restraint enforced socially rather than technologically.

Ethiopia dominates global marathons (69-74 of top 100 in 2025). Their method isn't "old school"—it's what Britt Rusert calls *fugitive science*: embodied empirical practice that doesn't register as "scientific" because it's not quantified.

Key tension: both methods are *checks on maladaptive excess* (overtraining). One social, one technological. Taken to extremes, the Ethiopian approach pulls individuals too far from their own growth trajectory; the Norwegian approach leaves athletes "too fragile to the turbulent demands of reality."

The essay ends with a quiet warning: as societies delegate intuition to self-tracking, thinking to AI, discipline to pharmaceuticals, "the scope of their measurements and guidance will always eventually be eclipsed by the totality of being human."

## Core Ideas

1. **Energy as social substance.** Ethiopian runners see energy as limited, shared, flowing between bodies. "Running alone is seen as deeply antisocial in the same way that eating alone is." Leading the group = carrying others' burden. Distributing pacemaking = ethics of energetic balance.

2. **Fugitive science.** East African runners are approached by researchers as *data sources*, not as people with in-depth knowledge about endurance. Their expertise is invisible to Western epistemology because it's not encoded in numbers.

3. **Restraint > suffering.** Both methods converge on this: the best athletes are obsessed with *not pushing too hard*, not with suffering more. Social media messaging ("pain cave") is noise. Real expertise = knowing when to hold back.

4. **Calibrating intuition vs. replacing it.** The golden mean: use engineering tools to *calibrate* intuition, not to supersede it. Create space for collective dynamics to stretch perceived limits. Neither pure data nor pure feel—the productive tension between them.

5. **Dangerousness and fun.** Zeleke and Gojjam get up at 3 AM to run hills in darkness, take cold showers. Not optimized. Not Goldilocks-zoned. Running as adventure, not protocol. The sleep-tracker's horror is the runner's joy.

## What Stays With Me

The image of Bogale leading a run through eucalyptus forest, refusing to speed up despite the GPS watch showing too few kilometers, telling the group "with this kind of training the important thing is to 'go up and down'"—then lying to the coach that the watch was broken. The act of *actively rejecting the measurement device* isn't anti-science. It's a different epistemology asserting itself: the body's knowledge of terrain > the watch's knowledge of distance.

Also: "my soul almost came out." Leading a group run is described in the language of near-death. Energy expenditure as spiritual risk, not metabolic math.
